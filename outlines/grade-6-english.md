## PEDAGOGICAL APPROACH

### Theoretical Foundation
The syllabus is based on the view that **learners are agents** - active users of English rather than passive recipients.

### Teaching Methods Used
1. **Constructivism** - Students construct their own learning
2. **Socio-constructivism** - Learning through social interaction
3. **Cognitivism** - Focus on mental processes
4. **Action-oriented approach** - Learning by doing
5. **Discovery Learning** - Students discover concepts themselves
6. **Total Physical Response (TPR)** - Physical movement in learning
7. **Humanistic approaches** - Student-centered, emotional engagement

## LESSON STRUCTURE

Every lesson follows a consistent structure with these sections:

### 1. **Let's Get Started** (Warm-up)
- Sets lesson context
- Activates prior knowledge (schemata)
- Uses brainstorming, spidergrams
- Can be done in pairs, groups, or whole-class

### 2. **Let's Listen / Let's Read** (Input)
- Silent reading is emphasized for comprehension
- First question targets GIST (main idea) - students skim
- Other questions develop SCANNING skills (specific information)
- Peer feedback is encouraged
- Students should respond in complete sentences, not isolated words

### 3. **Let's Complete the Rule** (Grammar Discovery)
- Grammar taught in context (usage + use)
- Students observe examples from reading/listening
- Teacher guides students to conceptualize and complete the rule
- Students then practice the structure

### 4. **Let's Practise** (Controlled Practice)
- Activities to consolidate target grammar/functions
- Meaningful and communicative contexts
- Students reinvest structures in production activities

### 5. **Let's Speak** (Production)
- Role plays and simulations are common
- Real-life scenarios
- Teacher demonstrates first (scaffolding)
- Students work with different partners
- Teacher monitors but doesn't interrupt

### 6. **Let's Write** (Written Production)
- Develops writing sub-skills
- Different genres: posts, emails, notes, paragraphs
- Clear purpose and audience
- Language support provided (scaffolding)
- Self-feedback and peer feedback encouraged
- Use of checklists and rubrics

### 7. **Let's Pronounce** (Pronunciation)
- Focus on specific sounds with phonetic symbols
- Techniques: minimal pairs, word classification, tongue twisters
- Process: Listen → Say aloud → Complete activity

### 8. **Let's Play** (Games/Consolidation)
- Board games common
- Roll dice, move spaces, answer questions/describe pictures
- Reinforces learning in a fun way
- Clear rules and time limits set

### 9. **Project Idea** (Extended Tasks)
- At end of each unit
- Posters, presentations, videos, stories, diaries
- Individual, pair, or group work
- Develops autonomy and collaboration
- Peer evaluation using evaluation grids

## PRONUNCIATION FOCUS

### Throughout the Units, Students Learn:

**Consonant Sounds:**
- /s/ vs. /z/ (cinema vs. zoo)
- /t/ vs. /d/ vs. /id/ (helped vs. loved vs. wanted)
- /ʃ/ vs. /ʧ/ (ship vs. cheap)
- /ð/ (the, weather, mother)
- /Ʒ/ vs. /ʤ/ (garage vs. jump)
- Silent 'h' (hour, school vs. house, happy)
- Silent 'k' (knife, know, knee)
- /w/ sound (winter, water, week)

**Vowel Sounds:**
- /i/ vs. /i:/ (fit vs. feet)
- /ai/ vs. /i/ (rice vs. fit)
- /ei/ sound (day, say, play)
- /ai/ sound (why, fly, my)

**Pronunciation Activities:**
- Minimal pairs
- Tongue twisters
- Word classification
- Listen and repeat (choral and individual drilling)

### Types of Writing Taught:

1. **Paragraphs**
   - Descriptive paragraphs (about towns, seasons, pets)
   - Narrative paragraphs (about events, trips)

2. **Emails**
   - Informal emails to friends
   - Proper layout: greeting, body, closing
   - Clear purpose and audience

3. **Messages/Notes**
   - Short messages
   - Thank you notes

4. **Lists**
   - Shopping lists
   - Activity lists

5. **Posters**
   - Health posters
   - Classroom rules/charter

### Writing Process:
1. **Pre-writing:** Brainstorming, planning
2. **Drafting:** Writing first draft
3. **Feedback:** Self-feedback, peer feedback, teacher feedback
4. **Revision:** Improving based on feedback
5. **Final version**

### Key Writing Skills:
- Sentence structure
- Punctuation (capital letters, full stops, question marks)
- Using linkers (and, but, because, first, then, finally)
- Appropriate vocabulary
- Staying on topic
- Writing for specific purpose and audience

## ASSESSMENT STRATEGIES

### Types of Assessment Used:

1. **Formative Assessment (Ongoing)**
   - Observation during activities
   - Peer feedback
   - Self-assessment
   - Teacher questioning
   - Homework checks

2. **Progress Checks**
   - At end of each unit
   - Tests reading comprehension, language, and writing
   - Covers grammar, vocabulary, and skills from unit

3. **Peer Evaluation**
   - Using checklists
   - Using rubrics
   - For presentations, writing, speaking

4. **Self-Evaluation**
   - Reflection on own learning
   - Identifying strengths and areas for improvement

### Progress Check Components:

**Reading Comprehension:**
- Read a text
- Answer comprehension questions
- True/False statements
- Matching activities

**Language:**

## TEACHING RECOMMENDATIONS

### For Successful Lessons:

1. **Clear Instructions**
   - Use simple sentences
   - Demonstrate activities
   - Use ICQs (Instruction-Checking Questions)
   - Provide examples

2. **Scaffolding**
   - Break complex tasks into steps
   - Provide language support (word banks, sentence starters)
   - Model expected output
   - Use visuals and realia

3. **Differentiation**
   - Group work allows peer support
   - Different learners can contribute at their level
   - Extension activities for fast finishers
   - Additional support for struggling learners

4. **Classroom Management**
   - Vary pair/group compositions
   - Give clear time limits
   - Monitor activities
   - Minimize teacher talk time, maximize student talk time

5. **Feedback**
   - Constructive and encouraging
   - Focus on content first, then form
   - Use peer feedback and self-assessment
   - Provide timely feedback

6. **Error Correction**
   - Don't interrupt during fluency activities
   - Note errors for later correction
   - Use positive error correction techniques
   - Focus on errors that impede communication

7. **Motivation**
   - Use games and fun activities
   - Connect to learners' interests and experiences
   - Celebrate successes
   - Create a supportive, non-threatening environment
   - Use praise appropriately
